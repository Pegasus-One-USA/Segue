3. The Platform and Data-Flow Architecture

3.1 Function

The Platform operates as an integration and transmission layer. It connects to a Source, retrieves records that Client is authorized to access, applies the field mappings and transformation rules configured by Client, and writes the resulting records to a Destination within the Client Environment.

3.2 Transient Processing

The Platform Processes ePHI in the course of extraction, transformation, and delivery. Except for operational metadata, audit records, and short-lived caches and queues necessary to perform the integration (each secured as described in Section 6), the Platform is not designed to serve as the system of record or long-term repository for Destination Data.

3.3 Client-Directed Delivery

All Destination Data is delivered to the location(s) that Client selects and configures. Provider does not determine which records are delivered, to which Destination, or in what form; those determinations are made solely through Client's configurations, mappings, data-group selections, and delivery settings.

6. Technical Safeguards (45 C.F.R. § 164.312)

Provider represents that the Platform implements, and Provider shall maintain, the technical safeguards described in this Section, which correspond to the standards of the Security Rule. A control-level summary is set forth in Exhibit B.

6.1 Access Control (§ 164.312(a))

  • Role-based access control. Access to sensitive functions and ePHI is restricted through enforced authorization and granular permission policies. No unauthenticated path to ePHI is exposed.
  • Unique user identification. Each authenticated action is attributable to a unique, named user identity; shared or generic service accounts are not used for user activity.
  • Automatic logoff. The administrative portal enforces an idle-session timeout, and access tokens expire after a defined interval in production environments.
  • Strong authentication policy. Local authentication enforces a minimum twelve (12) character password with mixed character classes, and supports enforced password rotation and multi-factor authentication.
  • Encryption and de-identification. Access controls are complemented by encryption and configurable de-identification, as described in Sections 8 and 10.

6.2 Audit Controls (§ 164.312(b))

  • Tamper-evident audit log. Configuration and entity changes are recorded in a durable, append-only audit log secured with a cryptographic (SHA-256) hash chain, designed to make undetected alteration evident.
  • Runtime pipeline audit trail. Pipeline extraction, transformation, and output events are recorded in a durable audit trail that persists across process restarts and does not embed PHI payloads.
  • Access logging. The Platform records both authorization denials and successful access events at the resource level, enabling review of who accessed which resources and when.
  • Correlation and traceability. Events are associated with correlation identifiers so that a specific transaction can be traced across the system.

6.3 Integrity (§ 164.312(c))

The hash-chained audit log and append-only guards provide a mechanism to corroborate that recorded configuration and entity data have not been improperly altered or destroyed. Diagnostic tracing is configured to exclude request/response bodies, headers, and query parameter values, capturing only correlation identifiers.

6.4 Person or Entity Authentication (§ 164.312(d))

The Platform authenticates users through the credential and token controls described in Section 6.1 and authenticates to Sources using industry-standard OAuth 2.0 / SMART-on-FHIR flows, including public client with PKCE, client secret, or private-key JWT, as configured for the applicable Source.

6.5 Transmission Security (§ 164.312(e))

  • Encryption in transit. HTTPS is enforced for the application and portal, including HTTP Strict Transport Security and required-HTTPS metadata, outside of local development environments.
  • Secure delivery channels. File delivery over SFTP uses an encrypted SSH channel with credentials sourced from a secret store; message-broker and cache transports enforce Transport Layer Security; and any HL7 MLLP transport, where enabled, is secured with TLS.
  • Origin restriction. Cross-origin access is restricted through a managed allowlist of permitted origins rather than an open policy.

7. Administrative and Organizational Safeguards (45 C.F.R. § 164.308)

7.1 Security Program

7.3 Risk Analysis

Provider conducts and maintains a periodic risk analysis of the potential risks and vulnerabilities to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of ePHI, and maintains a corresponding risk-treatment plan.

7.4 Workforce Training and Sanctions

Provider trains its workforce on its security and privacy policies and applies appropriate sanctions against workforce members who fail to comply.

7.5 Evaluation

Provider periodically evaluates the effectiveness of its safeguards through technical evaluation, which may include independent penetration testing of a non-production environment using synthetic data.

7.6 Contingency Planning

Provider maintains and periodically tests backup and disaster-recovery procedures for the components under its control, with documented recovery objectives.

8. Encryption and Key Management

8.1 Encryption at Rest

Secrets, access tokens, and generated output files handled within Provider-controlled components are encrypted at rest. Destination-connection secrets are stored in encrypted form or in a managed key vault; cached Source access tokens are encrypted; and generated file exports are encrypted prior to being written to disk and are subject to automated expiry and purge.

8.2 Encryption in Transit

All network transports carrying ePHI within Provider-controlled components enforce Transport Layer Security or an equivalent encrypted channel, as described in Section 6.5.

8.3 Key Management

Cryptographic keys and the data-protection key ring are themselves protected (for example, by certificate-based protection or a managed key vault) and are not stored in unprotected form. Signing secrets are provisioned automatically and may be rotated; rotation of a signing secret invalidates existing sessions.

8.4 Client-Side Encryption

Encryption of Destination Data at rest within the Client Environment, including transparent database encryption and storage-level encryption, is the responsibility of Client. The Platform surfaces a health check to flag an unencrypted target database, but does not administer the Client Environment.

9. Audit Controls and Compliance Reporting

9.1 Immutable Audit Records

The Platform maintains an immutable, hash-chained record of configuration and entity changes, capturing the actor, module, action, affected entity, status, and timestamp of each event.

9.2 Execution History

The Platform records the execution history of each pipeline run, including status, duration, and the identity of the triggering actor or schedule.

9.3 Evidence Reports

The Platform can generate compliance evidence reports for a selected date range, including audit-trail integrity verification, authentication activity, data-access summaries, security-event summaries, and error summaries, suitable for supporting an evaluation under 45 C.F.R. § 164.312(b).

9.4 Availability to Client

Client may access the audit, execution-history, and compliance-reporting functions applicable to its deployment, subject to the role-based permissions Client assigns to its users.

10. De-Identification and Minimum Necessary

10.1 De-Identification Capability

The Platform provides configurable de-identification services, including a Safe Harbor method and a k-anonymity method, that may be applied to a Destination on a per-Destination basis in accordance with Client's governance configuration.

10.2 Client Configuration

Whether a given Destination receives identified or de-identified data is a governance decision configured by Client. Client is responsible for determining, in consultation with its own compliance function, which Destinations require de-identification and which require identified data to function.

10.3 Minimum Necessary

The Platform's data-group and field-mapping controls allow Client to limit the categories and fields of ePHI delivered to a Destination. Client is responsible for configuring these controls consistent with the minimum-necessary standard and its own policies.

11. Client Responsibilities

Client acknowledges that the security of ePHI depends on the shared configuration and operation of the Platform and the Client Environment. Client shall:

  • maintain all authorizations, consents, and BAAs required to access and Process data from each Data Source Organization, and comply with those agreements;
  • provision, secure, encrypt, monitor, patch, back up, and administer the Client Environment, including all Destinations and the network, storage, and access controls applicable to Destination Data at rest;
  • configure the Platform appropriately, including Source and Destination connections, data groups, field mappings, transformation and de-identification rules, delivery settings, and upsert keys;
  • administer user accounts, roles, and permissions within the Platform, applying least-privilege principles and using the built-in roles (SuperAdmin, Admin, Operations, Audit) or custom roles as appropriate;
  • safeguard credentials, tokens, and secrets under Client's control, and configure permitted origins and allowed recipients responsibly; and
  • promptly notify Provider of any suspected security incident affecting the Platform or the Processing of ePHI.
